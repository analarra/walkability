WALKABILITY INEQUALITIES ACROSS AND WITHIN LATIN AMERICAN CITIES: A SEGMENT-LEVEL PERCEPTION-BASED FRAMEWORK

This study proposes a comprehensive and adaptable framework for assessing walkability in Latin American cities, with a focus on capturing local perceptions and built environment features. We developed the WalkUScan index by identifying key walkability components through perception surveys and modelling techniques in Porto Alegre (Brazil), Barranquilla (Colombia), and Concepción (Chile). The index was then applied using built environment data collected through virtual audits, public sources, and secondary data, enabling segment-level mapping of walkability conditions. These include perceived security, such as the presence of security cameras or personnel, adequate street lighting, and high pedestrian activity (more people walking); sidewalk quality, including the absence of cracks or surface damage, infrastructure that accommodates people with disabilities (e.g., ramps or tactile paving), and clearly delimited pedestrian paths; and traffic safety, such as the presence of traffic control devices or marked crosswalks, and low vehicle speed in pedestrian areas. Econometric analyses revealed inter- and intra-urban disparities in walkability. A pooled ranking logit model with city-specific scale parameters showed that cross-city differences reflect both response-scale effects and substantive variation in pedestrian priorities, supporting the need for locally calibrated walkability indices. Spatial analyses of walkability indices allow for the identification of local priorities, supporting the design of targeted, context-sensitive interventions. To support implementation, we developed an interactive web platform and a practical toolkit that offer a replicable methodology for other urban contexts and contribute to the development of more inclusive and responsive walkability assessment tools in Latin America.
